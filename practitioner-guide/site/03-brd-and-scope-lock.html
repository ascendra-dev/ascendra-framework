Stage 3

BRD & Scope Lock

The BRD is the contract between Product and Engineering. Once /review-brd sets it Approved, every requirement becomes a fixed unit of work that epics and stories will trace back to by REQ-ID — and every feature-area heading becomes an epic boundary you don't get to redraw for free later.

Process decision

Live session, AI-synthesis, or skip to /gen-brd?

run-brd-discovery.md assumes a two-role interview. If you have no client, you have to enact AI-synthesis-and-review yourself, inside the same command.

Scores you can't verify

The denominator is topics and journeys together — Section 4 and Section 6 combined, not Section 4 alone, regardless of what the command's own wording might suggest.

Coverage is not resolution. A "Covered" mark means a topic was asked and answered — not that the answer was a real decision. A client who says "it depends" to everything can rack up 100% coverage on a nearly content-free BRD. When an answer is genuine uncertainty, mark it Pending and open a Section 7 entry with a real owner — even at ≥85%, re-check for scope-blocking Open Questions before generating.

Decision heuristic

Known Variation vs. an ordinary follow-up

Does the answer fit inside a topic subsection that already exists, or does it need a topic that isn't there?The test

If it's an unusual value on an axis that already exists, resolve it in the session, write it down, and move on. If it's a structurally new axis the domain knowledge doesn't model at all, flag it for a domain knowledge update rather than just a BRD note — otherwise the next project in this domain will hit the exact same gap.

The circuit breaker

Two signals mean the problem isn't discovery — it's that you're talking to someone who can't give a stable answer: the same topic revised more than twice across sessions, or a Scope Risk Flag flipping status more than once. When either happens, stop scheduling sessions and escalate to a decision-maker above the immediate contact.

Density guidance — the single most important skill in this phase

REQ-writing density

One REQ = one independently testable, independently story-able capability.The rule
✓ Correctly dense — REQ-002
"The system must detect duplicate Payers on registration. The deduplication key is configurable per organisation: phone number, email, or both. Default is phone number."

Three sentences, but every clause is a facet of one capability. Strip any part out and a developer has to ask which key to check.

✗ Over-fat — REQ-BAD-FAT
"...create an invoice with line items and a due date, submit it for Director approval if the total is above the threshold, and generate a PDF once approved."

Welds together three independently shippable capabilities — creation, approval, PDF generation — each with its own real REQ in the actual BRD.

✗ Over-thin — REQ-BAD-THIN
"Invoices above a threshold require Director approval."

Passes a superficial "short enough" read but silently omits the number that makes it usable. A developer can't write a single AC from this.

The test: could each half of the sentence ship, get tested, and get demoed on its own, with the other half simply not built yet? If yes to both, split. If the second half is meaningless without the first, fold it in.

Full working depth

Scope vs. Priority vs. Layer

Should Have vs. Out of Scope — deferred

Should Have is a Priority value — it only applies to something already In Scope, and it ships this phase if time allows. Out of Scope — deferred is different: the item simply isn't being built this phase at all.

§5.21 vs. §15 Parking Lot

Can you write one factual sentence backed by the domain doc or this session? If yes, §5.21 (gets a REQ-ID). If you're guessing, §15 (no fields until promoted).

Can you write one factual sentence describing exactly how it will behave, backed by something already in the domain knowledge document or this discovery session? If yes, Section 5.21. If you're guessing, Section 15.FW-024

Downstream impact

What the BRD commits you to: the epic boundary you're already drawing

Each Section 5.N feature-area heading becomes one epic, 1:1, when /gen-epics runs. Choose feature-area boundaries the way you'd choose epic boundaries — not chapter headings. Harborview's five feature areas are five genuinely independent capabilities; that's why they became five clean epics.

Could this feature area ship and be demoed as a complete, independently useful capability on its own?Ask this of every Section 5 heading
What "good" looks like

Reviewing and approving

You should be able to restate what each requirement will actually produce, in your own words, without looking back at the REQ text. By the end of the review, Section 16's Flags table should be empty or fully resolved, there should be no unresolved scope blockers, and every [Assumed] tag should have actually been looked at, not just scrolled past.

The gap: what to do when you don't disagree, you just don't know. Don't force yourself into Confirm/Change/Remove. Tell the AI you're uncertain and want it routed to Section 12 (Open Questions) with the client as owner — a legitimate outcome of the review, not a failure to complete it.

Terminology recap
  • §5.21 vs. §15 Parking LotBoth hold Out of Scope items, but only 5.21 has domain/discovery grounding and a REQ-ID.
  • Requirement Confidence ScoreA coverage measure, not a resolution measure. Don't conflate the two.
Common mistakes ▸
  • Marking a topic Covered when the real answer was "it depends" or "I don't know."
  • Writing a REQ that's really two or three capabilities glued together with "and."
  • Writing a REQ so thin it omits the number, threshold, or default that makes it usable.
  • Confusing Should Have with Out of Scope — deferred.
  • Putting an ungrounded idea into §5.21 because it's convenient, instead of the honest §15.
  • Noting a structurally new domain variation as just a BRD aside instead of flagging a domain knowledge update.
  • Letting a feature-area heading sprawl into a "Miscellaneous" catch-all.
  • Approving a BRD with [Assumed] tags nobody actually re-examined.
  • Scheduling a fourth or fifth discovery session on the same unresolved topic instead of escalating.
