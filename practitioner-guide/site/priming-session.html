Priming Session

Every real discovery session starts from a blank page unless something fills it first. /run-priming-session is that fill — a free-form, optional, repeatable conversation, with or without raw material dropped into source-material/, that materializes whatever facts already exist into one file before formal discovery ever starts. Nothing in it counts as confirmed; it's a head start, not a shortcut. If that distinction feels thin right now, keep reading — this chapter starts from zero.

What this session is for

What does it produce? One file, priming/priming-package.md, that /run-intake, /run-domain-discovery, and /run-brd-discovery all read as a head start when it exists. None of them require it, and none of them treat anything in it as confirmed — a priming package is provisional by design, re-confirmed for real once the actual discovery session reaches each topic.

Why isn't this just an informal discovery session? Because a rigid script forces structure onto raw, half-formed material before it's ready for one. There's no playbook, no fixed question order, and no requirement to cover everything here — priming exists to materialize facts cheaply, in whatever order they arrive, so the real sessions start from something instead of nothing.

Where does its content come from? Whatever the PO drops into source-material/, whatever comes up in free-form conversation, or both. Scoped to Problem Domain only — Brief, Domain, and BRD material — nothing here touches architecture or later stages.

Process decision

Two ways in, and when to use which

The session opens with one question: files, conversation, or both? The answer branches into two genuinely different halves.

Triaging dropped material

A three-pass pipeline — Map (skim, build an outline), Triage (mark each unit relevant/redundant/irrelevant/ambiguous, checking for disagreement, not just redundancy), Extract (deep-read only what survived, compressed, never a verbatim transcription).

The live conversation

No script, no fixed order. The PO raises whatever they want; the job is to listen for what maps onto the priming package's sections, not march through a checklist out loud.

If both files and conversation exist, triage the files first. It's cheaper to ask about genuine gaps once you know what the files already answered.Step 4's branch
Compression, not simplification

Question density

The PO is not a layman being onboarded — use domain terms and their own vocabulary freely.

✓ Correct
"Who signs off on the expensive invoices — anyone in particular, or any one of a group?"
✗ Incorrect
"I'd like to understand your organization's approval workflow in more detail — could you walk me through, in as much detail as you're comfortable with, exactly who is involved..."

Bounded question loops. Ask up to roughly three short questions on one topic. If it's still not resolving, stop asking and state a short draft instead: "sounds like — any one of the three directors can approve, no specific order — right?" Correcting a draft costs the PO less than continuing to answer questions, and it closes topics that would otherwise stall the whole session.

100% coverage is never the goal. If a topic genuinely doesn't come up, leave it Pending and move on — don't manufacture a question for every empty section just to fill it. A short, honest session that leaves real gaps clearly marked beats a long one padded out to look complete.

The one rule worth its own section

"Out of scope" governs asking, not noticing

Anything already Approved or Locked in a real artifact is out of scope for this session's own questions — don't re-ask about it. But that's a rule about what you go looking for, not about what you do when something surfaces anyway.

A genuine new fact that touches an already-settled topic still gets recorded — into Section 9 (Open/Uncategorized), naming the affected artifact and its status, not silently dropped because the topic is "out of scope."Otherwise a fact that should trigger /assess-change never reaches anyone
A real, worked case

Handling disagreement: the Conflicting Source flag

When a unit disagrees with something already captured — two places in one file, two files, or a file against something already said — never silently pick a side. Write both statements in full, each attributed to its origin, and tag the flag Conflicting Source. If the PO is live when it surfaces, ask directly, right then — cheaper resolution than deferring to a flag.

priming-package.example.md — Section 5

James stated, live, that VAT is "20%, always, no exceptions I can think of." But old-invoice-tracker.xlsx, one of the files he dropped in, shows a real historical invoice — INV-2025-000412 — at a 0% VAT line item. Nobody asked James about that specific invoice, and the spreadsheet gives no reason. The session recorded both statements, flagged Conflicting Source, and left it for /run-domain-discovery to resolve directly.

Two flags, two postures

Legacy material: correction vs. question

AI Knowledge Correction

The material diverges from generic domain understanding for a real, stated business reason. Carry it forward as a genuine fact about this client.

Legacy Design Question

It diverges and looks like a design flaw, not a real constraint. Don't carry it forward as fact — frame it as a negotiation and raise it with the PO directly.

priming-package.example.md — Section 5

James: "we use Stripe for basically everything else already, would make sense to use it here too." Not a generic domain default (some payment processor) — a stated preference, flagged AI Knowledge Correction precisely because a different client in the same domain might have said something else entirely.

Before you write anything

Density check: every entry should read like a discovery-state file's "PO stated" line — one to three sentences. If an entry reads like it belongs in the actual BRD or domain knowledge document, it's too dense — compress it back down. This is the same discipline Chapter 3 taught you for a REQ, applied one phase earlier at a lower bar: a priming package entry is provisional, and writing it at final-artifact density overstates how settled it actually is.

Terminology recap
  • Covered / Partial / PendingA priming topic's status values. Partial surfaces as an informed live question later — never silently treated as complete.
  • Section 9 vs. Section 10Section 9 holds genuine one-off leftovers. Section 10 is reserved for a whole expected category of fact from a different extraction mechanic — this command owns no subsection there at all.
  • No Ubiquitous Language yetCapture the PO's own terms as used. If a concept gets two names, flag it in Section 7 — resolving it is domain discovery's job, later.
Common mistakes ▸
  • Re-asking about a topic that's already Approved or Locked, instead of checking first.
  • Silently dropping a genuine new fact because it touches an already-settled topic, instead of routing it to Section 9.
  • Picking a side on a Conflicting Source disagreement instead of recording both statements with their origins.
  • Carrying forward a legacy quirk as a universal domain fact, without checking whether it's a real constraint or a design flaw.
  • Writing a priming entry at BRD or domain-document density instead of a one-to-three-sentence line.
  • Manufacturing a question for every empty section to look complete, instead of leaving a genuine gap Pending.
  • Treating a rushed "yes, that's right" the same as a real, reasoned answer.
